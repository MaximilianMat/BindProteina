Portpardelle-1c
“The binder design problem can be treated as a special case of the motif scaffolding problem, in which the target structure is the motif and the binder is the piece to generate. To handle multiple chains, we use learned relative positional embeddings added to the attention matrix analogous to AlphaFold2 [19], with a residue index gap between chains of 200. We train on a 50/50 mix of the single-chain augmented CATH dataset described previously and a curated chain pair dataset following Boltz-1 [20] (Appendix A).”A similar approach might be possible for LaProteina?Necessary changes:
different (additional) training set to adapt LaProteina to multimers
some structural changes needed, but possible
Next task? 2 possible approaches:
Explicitly add cross-chain pair features
Add a 200 res gap and learn that a large gap means different sequence (AF2 way)

Portpardelle-1c
“It is unclear whether the Protpardelle-1c samples are indeed difficult to pass in silico filters due to AF2 preference for helical structures” ~portpardelle-1c p.11Interesting: In the paper they try to benchmark Portpardelle against BindCraft and get worse results for 8 targets (measured in AF2 plDDt and ipTM). They argue that this might not necessarily result in worse in-vitro reproducibility, as BindCraft is optimized for AF2 scores => this could be similar for LaProteinaAlso they notice a preference in helicity for BindCraft, where Portpardelle seems to favour sheets for some samples. This might explain the worse AF2 scores (AF2 prefers Helical structures). So there might be plausible samples (that use sheets) that could have good in-vitro test results.
